About

From materials to quantum circuits

My interests lie in how the physics of a device shapes its behaviour in a larger quantum system.

I work as a Project Engineer in the Quantum Computing Group at C-DAC, Bengaluru, where I contribute to superconducting quantum processor development under India’s National Quantum Mission.

My work includes modelling coupled transmons and two-qubit gates, designing readout structures, and studying noise and decoherence. I use circuit models alongside electromagnetic simulation to connect target frequencies and coupling rates with physical geometry.

Education and research background

I completed an M.Tech in Semiconductor Technology at the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru (2023–2025), with a minor in Quantum Technology. My thesis, supervised by Prof. Pavan Nukala, investigated phase behaviour in VO₂ wires using Raman spectroscopy, substrate stretching and AFM nanoindentation.

Coursework and projects at IISc also gave me experience with semiconductor devices, fabrication, quantum optics and integrated photonics. Before IISc, I studied Mechanical Engineering at DCR University of Science and Technology, Sonipat (2019–2023).

What I want to work on

I’m interested in PhD research on superconducting quantum hardware, especially circuit QED, two-qubit control, readout and parametric amplification. I want to build on my modelling experience and work more closely with device experiments.

Outside my current role

I have worked on variational quantum algorithms during an internship at Entangled Ket and participated in quantum programming challenges. A few of those notebooks are linked alongside my research projects.
