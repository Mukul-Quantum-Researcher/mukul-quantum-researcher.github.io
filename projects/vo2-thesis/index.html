IISc · M.Tech thesis · 2025

Phase behaviour in VO₂ wires

Raman observations and the practical challenges of transferring stress to a small, stiff sample.

Read the report (PDF)

Research question

Can mechanical stress be used to change the phase of VO₂ wires? My M.Tech thesis at IISc, supervised by Prof. Pavan Nukala, investigated this using Raman spectroscopy, stretching on PDMS and AFM-based nanoindentation.

I prepared wires on glass, Si/SiO₂ and PDMS substrates, compared Raman spectra under different excitation conditions, and examined the response before and after mechanical loading.

Raman observations

The measurements showed a dependence on wire width and substrate. Wires below roughly 4 µm changed their Raman response at lower excitation settings than wider wires. Samples on glass generally showed a more direct M1-to-rutile-like response, while Si/SiO₂ and PDMS samples often showed intermediate characteristics.

These observations concern the response under optical excitation. Raman spectra alone do not constitute a direct measurement of an electrical metal–insulator transition.

What the mechanical experiments showed

Stretching the PDMS substrate did not produce clear Raman peak shifts. Poor adhesion allowed the wires to slide, so extending the substrate did not reliably transfer strain to the wire.

What I learned

The experiments did not demonstrate a controlled stress-induced phase transition. They identified two practical obstacles: getting strain into an individual wire and applying enough local force with a suitable probe.

The next experiments would need better mechanical anchoring, direct loading of individual wires, or a stiffer probe or dedicated nanoindenter. The main outcome was a clearer account of the measurement conditions and mechanical limits that such an experiment needs to address.
