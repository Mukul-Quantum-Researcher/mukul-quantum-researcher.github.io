Microwave design · Qiskit Metal & HFSS

Purcell-protected readout

Implementing an intrinsic-notch readout structure and tuning its simulated frequency through geometry.

Code & documentation

The design problem

A wider readout linewidth can improve readout speed while opening a stronger Purcell decay channel. I explored a coupled readout/filter structure in which a multiconductor transmission-line section creates an intrinsic notch in the transfer impedance.

The design follows the MTL-coupled filter approach of Spring et al. My work was to implement the geometry in Qiskit Metal, transfer it to HFSS and iterate the shorted-end lengths on my chosen stack.

Geometry and EM workflow

I modelled a feedline, Purcell filter and readout resonator, with lumped ports for driven-modal S- and Z-parameter sweeps. Changing the shorted-end sections moved the notch while retaining the coupled-resonator topology.

Moving the notch

The supplied results show a notch at approximately 11.42 GHz in the initial design, 8.35 GHz after lengthening the shorted sections, and 6.8256 GHz in the Project14 plot. This demonstrates frequency tuning within the simulated geometry.

What is available

The repository contains four HFSS design files, two CSV exports, selected plots and a geometry notebook from the later Project15 variant. The geometry notebook is labelled separately because it does not regenerate Project14 exactly. Raw solver caches are excluded.

The middle-notch CSV has “project 12” in its original filename, while the presentation labels the corresponding stage Project13. That naming mismatch is preserved in the provenance notes rather than silently reassigned.

Limits

These are electromagnetic simulations. I have not established measured readout fidelity, measured qubit lifetime or fabrication performance from these files. The performance numbers cited from the reference paper are not results of my implementation.

Full reproduction requires a compatible Qiskit Metal environment and licensed Ansys HFSS. The exported CSV analysis can run independently of HFSS.
