Cross-resonance and echoed CR gates

Connecting a coupled-transmon model to pulse sequences and circuit design targets.

Code & documentation

The question

For two fixed-frequency transmons, how do frequency detuning, coupling and drive strength affect a cross-resonance gate? I built a workflow that starts with the static spectrum and follows through to a calibrated echoed sequence.

What I implemented

The original tutorial uses scqubits for transmon spectra and QuTiP for lab-frame dynamics. A second notebook builds the models directly in QuTiP: exact-cosine transmons for static checks, and a three-level Duffing model for pulse dynamics.

The workflow calculates dressed levels, static ZZ and effective CR rates; compares rotating-frame and lab-frame trajectories; scans detuning and coupling; and calibrates a CR(+)–X(π)–CR(−) sequence. It also exports approximate circuit targets for later layout and EM extraction.

Selected simulation result

For the selected example—4.9 and 5.0 GHz qubits, −300 MHz anharmonicities and 4 MHz exchange coupling—the notebook reports an approximately 154 ns ECR sequence, with coherent average gate fidelity around 99.685% after virtual-Z optimisation and average leakage around 5.7 × 10⁻⁵.

These are model results, not measured device performance. The fidelity is evaluated against the ideal ECR on the computational block, with leakage reducing the reported fidelity.

What the model leaves out

The pulse simulation uses three levels per transmon. It omits DRAG, target rotary drive, direct-drive crosstalk, readout resonators, thermal population and detailed TLS or flux noise. The lab-frame CR check agrees with the rotating-frame example to roughly one percent in populations.

A Lindblad example compares one input state with and without T₁/T₂ decay. That state fidelity is not an estimate of the noisy average gate fidelity. The exported coupling capacitance is a first-pass estimate that still needs EM verification.
