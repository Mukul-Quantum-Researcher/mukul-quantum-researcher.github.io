Qubit spectroscopy and Rabi chevrons

Connecting a driven transmon model to frequency sweeps, pulse-duration scans and fitted dynamics.

Code & documentation

What I modelled

I built a four-level transmon simulation to explore frequency-domain spectroscopy and time-domain Rabi measurements. The model includes a flat-top Gaussian drive, T₁/T₂ relaxation and dephasing, finite-temperature excitation and Gaussian quasi-static frequency offsets.

I swept drive frequency and pulse duration, averaged simulated trajectories, and fitted the resulting chevron. The example uses a 5.011 GHz transition, −500 MHz anharmonicity, T₁ = 40 µs and T₂ = 35 µs. These are chosen model parameters, not measured device values.

Spectroscopy through a chevron

Joint frequency and duration data provide more structure than a coarse frequency sweep alone. Re-fitting the 180 saved simulated points recovers a transition near 5011.03 MHz and a Rabi frequency near 5.00 MHz, close to the example’s input values.

Rabi oscillations

A pulse-duration sweep provides a second view of the driven dynamics. A separate fit using several starting points gives approximately 5.00 MHz for the saved Rabi data, consistent with the chevron fit.

What needs care

The original 10 MHz frequency sweep is too coarse to support its narrow fitted linewidth, and the Lorentzian function contains an unused phase parameter. I do not use that fit as a linewidth or frequency-accuracy result. The original Rabi fit output also disagrees with the recorded oscillations; the separate analysis script records the corrected fit.

The frequency noise is quasi-static Gaussian sampling, not a full 1/f-noise spectrum. The voltage-to-drive factor is assumed, so this is preparation for characterisation and calibration work rather than a measured FPGA or qubit calibration.
