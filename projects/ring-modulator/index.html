Integrated photonics · IISc course project

A silicon ring modulator

Linking waveguide optics, carrier depletion and a voltage-dependent ring resonance.

Read the report (PDF)

Design brief

For my integrated photonics course project, I designed a silicon-on-insulator ring modulator around 1550 nm. The assignment specified more than 15 dB output swing for a 3 V peak-to-peak signal applied to a reverse-biased PN junction.

I worked through the optical and electrical parts of the design: waveguide modes in MODE, coupling in FDTD, depletion in CHARGE and ring transmission in INTERCONNECT.

Passive resonator

A 450 nm waveguide width supported the selected TE mode in the model. The radius sweep placed a resonance near 1550 nm at approximately 9 µm. A coupling coefficient of 0.0076 gave a reported passive Q of about 58,000 and an FSR of about 11 nm.

From depletion to optical response

I simulated a PN junction with nominal doping of 2.5 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³, examined depletion over the DC reverse-bias range and used the resulting carrier profiles in the optical mode model. The calculated effective index was then used in the circuit-level ring model.

Result and limits

The report documents passive-resonator optimisation and a voltage-dependent optical response. It does not establish a measured device, RF bandwidth, or a demonstrated 3 V peak-to-peak modulation swing. The DC spectra are kept separate from that design target.

The tuning pages consistently use a radius close to 9 µm; the 0.89997 µm value on the summary slide is inconsistent with that sweep. This page follows the sweep. The report also gives conflicting explanations of loss versus bias, so I have not promoted a causal claim about Q degradation.

This page describes my independently completed simulation work. The supplied report is available in its original form. Native Lumerical simulation files were not included in the source material.
