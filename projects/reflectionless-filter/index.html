JTWPA gain with reflectionless filtering

Comparing the effect of external impedance on the same amplifier, with and without a reflectionless filter.

Code & documentation

The question

How does the impedance of a readout chain affect the gain of a Josephson travelling-wave parametric amplifier? I compared the same amplifier with ideal 50 Ω loads, nonideal circulator models, and a reflectionless-filter/circulator cascade.

The study is motivated by Kedziora et al.’s reflectionless-filter work. The JTWPA circuit and parameters come from the JosephsonCircuits example based on Macklin et al. My work is the model comparison and integration of the external termination impedances.

What I implemented

I used a 2,048-cell JTWPA driven by a 7.12 GHz, 1.85 µA pump, and swept the small-signal response from 1 to 14 GHz. The amplifier core stays fixed across the three cases.

The circulator and filter are described by surrogate S-parameter functions. I reduced each external network to the complex input reflection and then to a frequency-dependent impedance at the amplifier port. The nonlinear pump solve and small-signal calculation therefore see the external loading.

Understanding the comparison

The filtered model lies closer to the ideal-load gain baseline than the circulator-only model. The external network’s complex reflection, including its out-of-band behaviour, matters alongside its passband transmission.

The reflection plot below makes the assumed environments visible. These curves are generated by the surrogate functions in the notebook; they are not measured circulator or filter data.

Validation and limits

The original notebook retains its solver warning for the circulator case. A separate Python check samples the surrogate networks from 1 to 14 GHz, checking positive real input impedances and a contractive filter scattering matrix at those points. No fresh harmonic-balance run is claimed.

This is a simulation study using surrogate networks. It is not an exact reproduction of the paper’s measured-data comparison or a measured amplifier result. Quantum efficiency and thermal-noise performance were not calculated, and the port-impedance normalisation must be considered when comparing S-parameter gain across cases.
